# Twenty-Paper Tournament

This tournament is deliberately an elimination exercise. The literature and
frozen evidence rule out a definitive or top-tier thesis: canonicalization
obligations, downstream semantic gaps, JSON differential testing, and receipt
architectures already have strong prior art, while this record lacks a
maintained receipt relying party, a second population, and independent
reproduction.

Scores use the requested 100-point rubric: novelty 18, research question 12,
evidence 14, implementation alignment 10, ecological validity 10, reviewer
survivability 10, significance 8, depth 6, reproducibility 4, scope discipline
4, and coherence 4. They are decision aids, not measurements. A fatal gate
overrides a score; a score below 85 cannot justify a top-tier readiness claim.

## Final cut

**Top five candidates:** P01, P03, P02, P06, and P04. Their evidence belongs
in one narrowly scoped note, rather than five separate papers.

**Final three:** P01, P03, and P02. P01 wins because it is the only thesis that
can carry both the controlled decision result and the adverse ecological result
without pretending either is broader than it is. P03 becomes the instrument and
P02 becomes the main negative finding.

**Fatal gates:**

- P19 and P20 fail implementation alignment.
- P12 fails real-agent and architecture novelty.
- P15 and P18 lack a non-elementary, observed formal result.
- P03, P09, P14, and P17 lack the external evaluation a tool/profile thesis
  needs.
- P02 cannot be elevated to prevalence from 64 eligible entries and 16
  producers.
- P11 cannot be elevated from author-supplied policy-engine outcomes to
  deployed receipt-consumer behavior.
- P05 has no all-default implementation example.

The maximum score is 66. The conclusion is not “submit a weak main-track
paper”; it is “make the narrow note rigorously reproducible, then earn the
right to reassess with one independent ecosystem study.”
